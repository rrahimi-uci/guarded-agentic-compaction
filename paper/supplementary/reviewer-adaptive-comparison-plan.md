## Question and scope

Does automatically guarded prefix compilation improve the task-quality/resource
trade-off over simpler compilation when a workflow requires adaptive decisions after
a recurrent retrieval opening? Separate two estimands: the effect of individual
safeguards on a fixed compiled region, and the value of the compiler's selection
policy over a common candidate list. A shorter prefix in one arm is a policy
difference, not a clean ablation of one safeguard.

The first source-feasibility target is BIRD SQL tasks because the repository already
has read-only database execution and a task-result grader independent of tool-path
imitation. SQL construction and table selection exercise adaptive decisions; schema
retrieval supplies a possible reusable opening. This is a proposed target, not proof
that enough fresh independent groups or distinct candidate families exist. The
schema-first result already in the paper must not be repackaged as this experiment.

If BIRD cannot supply the required source/task variation and untouched groups, stop
before provider discovery. The alternative is complete fresh GitHub issue/PR evidence
with an independently specified semantic task and blinded labels. Metadata routing,
placeholder comments, and unvalidated model-judge labels do not meet that alternative.

The static SQL contract mapping is feasible; the live Agent JIT runtime, equal-information
tools, candidate accounting and isolation are not implemented. The old per-database

weighting, grouping, and error allocation before selecting its final sample size.

Historical final-answer error in the admitted BIRD cohorts is about 35–36%, so a
positive-coverage 5% absolute final-error certificate cannot be assumed. If a new study
instead targets quality loss relative to the unchanged agent, define that paired loss
prospectively and label its certificate accordingly. It is not an absolute success
guarantee or a reinterpretation of the old replay certificate. Do not choose the endpoint
after seeing the new results. The current decision remains no-go for confirmatory execution.

## Stage B: discovery and fixed candidates

Use fresh training groups to mine candidate prefixes under a declared task/tool
interface. Development groups select bounded synthesis settings, feature/model
choices, contracts, and a finite threshold grid. Do not inspect calibration or final
outcomes to select candidates, enrich easy classes, replace rejected candidates, or
choose a favorable task. Retain all candidate dispositions, including no-artifact
outcomes. Repository/database count is not a count of distinct workflow structures.

Freeze the common candidate list before GAC-specific rejection for the whole-compiler
comparison. For the fixed-region mechanism comparison, freeze the exact program,
continuation packaging, recovery tools, and grader before changing one restriction
at a time. Every arm enforces read-only effects and isolation; an unguarded baseline
is not permission to execute writes or arbitrary generated code unsandboxed.

## Conditions and outcomes

| Arm | Definition | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Unchanged agent | Same task, tools, model, and inference settings | Absolute task/resource reference |
| Full GAC | Frozen grounding, contracts, and statistical admission | Proposed policy |
| Recurrence-only compiler | Same train candidate pool; deterministic binding policy frozen before evaluation; unsupported cases fall back | Simpler whole-compiler reference |
| Support-only compiler | Same candidate pool and grounded programs; predeclared support rule, no learned ranking | Statistical-stage attribution |
| Handwritten composite | Fixed retrieval program with the same output packaging and continuation | Engineering upper/reference baseline |
| Named compiler | Version-pinned faithful implementation or explicitly documented adapter | Literature comparison |

For the mechanism subset, add only prespecified one-at-a-time removals that preserve
the program and recovery interface. Do not infer benefit from different prefix lengths.
Retain all rejected candidates as policy outcomes: a rejection is not an avoided error.

Primary endpoint: final-task failure among all scheduled groups, including provider
failures and failed recovery. Report total cost against that endpoint. Diagnostics:
dispatch coverage, wrong answers after dispatch, clean fallback, incidents, requests,
tokens, tools, latency, and measured setup/monitoring costs. Distinguish unknown failed
call usage from zero. Human authoring effort is recorded or declared unmeasured.

Ordinary tasks and stress tasks are separate strata. Stress changes must be specified
from a real tool failure mode (e.g. incomplete schema responses), preserve the task's
gold semantics, and apply identically across arms. Constructed stress does not establish
natural-drift prevalence. No favorable post hoc mixture of strata is permitted.

## Calibration repair and selective comparison

Sample calibration groups before observing baseline success or mined-family occurrence.
Freeze the candidate(s), hard guard, verifier, score, thresholds, loss, and multiplicity
budget first. Run the frozen prefix and continuation on every eligible group; grade
final outcomes independently of whether the baseline took the same tool path. Record
pre-dispatch rejection, clean fallback, interpreter/verifier failure and incident
separately. Final task errors after a fallback remain in the declared task loss.

No labels may be dropped for failed baseline runs, absent family windows, or model
timeouts. If an outcome is genuinely missing, use a prespecified conservative rule
or declare the certificate unavailable. Do not silently treat unknown as zero error.

For a 5% target at delta=0.10 and 11 thresholds, one candidate needs at least 92
accepted zero-error groups; two need 106. At 50% empirical coverage the corresponding
total pools need at least 184 or 212 even before allowing errors. These are arithmetic
floors, not adequate power or independence guarantees. A learned-gate claim requires
at least two positive certified operating points and comparison with a support-only
rule at matched coverage on the same untouched evaluation population. If those points
cannot be certified, report that outcome; do not extend the cohort after seeing it.

Independent final testing estimates quality/resource differences; it does not select
the final threshold. Prespecify primary paired contrasts, family-wise correction,
noninferiority margin if used, cluster sensitivities, stopping, and null/adverse result
interpretation. Multiple databases alone do not establish independence of questions.

## Execution seal and spending

The final seal must contain acquired source/ID hashes; trained candidate manifests;
all arm implementations; grader tests; exact power outputs; calibrated policies; final
test schedule with balanced arm order; pinned model/tool versions; timeout/retry and
interruption rules; measured pilot token costs; reserve; and a numeric spending cap.
Calibration and final-test phases require separate seals so final testing cannot
change a failed calibration decision. Discovery/pilot costs are separately bounded.

The current repository's historical price estimates are not a current quote. Once
Stage A supplies an executable scope, present the actual pilot schedule and budget
for authorization before paid calls. Approval of a research direction does not fill
in missing data, adapters, or statistical design.

## Completion criteria

- Publish all scheduled outcomes and dispositions, including null/adverse comparisons.
- Demonstrate a quality/cost advantage over the relevant simpler policies, or narrow
  the contribution. Do not define success as obtaining a favorable p-value.
- Make any certificate conditional on its actual population, grouping, and selection
  procedure. A replay result never becomes an end-to-end guarantee by wording.
- Update manuscript and reviewer response only from retained results; leave the PR
  open for author review and do not post or upload automatically.
