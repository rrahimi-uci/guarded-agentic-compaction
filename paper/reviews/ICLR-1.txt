Paper Summary:
The paper intorduces "guarded agentic compaction", a technique to allow "mature" agents to not have to repeat key decision-making steps unnecessarily. It adapts guarded tracing compilation to initial read-only agent prefixes, combining argument provenance, effect declarations and induced contracts with statistical admission (Sections 1-3). Clean failures return to the unchanged agent. The paper gives a conditional finite-sample selective-risk bound and reports 66.6% fewer provider requests and 63.1% fewer tokens across 90 paired GitHub cases, with 90/90 compiled and 89/90 baseline exact contracts (Table 1). External benchmarks establish compilation feasibility or refusal (without live-agent efficiency measurements).

Critical Strengths:
The paper connects guarded tracing compilation and bounded synthesis to agent-specific substitution hazards (Sections 1 and 6). Argument witnesses, declared effects and entry-position checks prevent distinct failures documented in Figure 1 and Table 20. This gives practitioners a useful account of which recurring decisions can be replaced and where the agent must retain control. In the workflow-reuse literature, Agent Workflow Memory induces routines to guide subsequent generations. GAC contributes a complementary deployment criterion for replacing those generations with executable programs. Connecting program synthesis to statistical admission is a useful idea.
Automatic derivation of useful programs, together with explicit failures and refusals, constitutes a practical contribution even when hand-written bundles match runtime performance (Table 9).
Critical Weaknesses:
The introduction is hard to follow and feels very LLM written. Concepts are introduced without context (e.g. ``held out issue #4420'': what kind of issue is this? is merely an example or it's part of a dataset that's introduced elsewhere? what's defined in this issue? what does it have to do at the start of the third paragraph without mentioning the notion of issue or why it matters to the paper?)
The benchmarks do not compare against alternative agentic workflow compilation methods that have been proposed in the literature (e.g. Guarded Programs: EvoC2F and Agent JIT Compilation). A comparison should assess the cost–quality trade-off against other compilers. The results in Table 2 are difficult to interpret and don't allow judging the quality of the compiled segments.
Section 2 includes downstream answer errors in the certified loss (p. 3), whereas the supplied calibration helper labels tool-output agreement after verifier success without evaluating the continuation. Section 7 acknowledges continuation limits, and Appendix F.2 shows that correct replay can precede an incorrect answer. It's not clear how the reported labels support the end-to-end certificate, which is central to the admissibility contribution (even perfect replay labels cannot bound unmeasured continuation errors). The authors should align the loss, labels and claims throughout: a replay-only certificate would support a narrower contribution, while the stated episode-level guarantee requires calibration of the substituted agent's final outcomes.
The calibration population also needs accounting. The primary study drivers select successful discovery traces before splitting, and the compiler constructs calibration samples from matching family windows. Every dispatch-eligible member sampled before recurrence filtering requires labels (p. 29): it's not clear whether omitted cases could pass the deployed guard. If they could, the calibration bound would describe a selected population rather than the claimed deployment population. If excluded records can pass the deployed guard, recalibration should sample before outcome filtering and retain failures. Enlarging the same filtered cohort would not repair this mismatch.
Post-deployment lifecycle policy is underspecified. Algorithm 4 checks active status and manifest compatibility, and the supplement implements expiry, retirement and rollback. What remains unclear is when accumulating failures or task-distribution changes should trigger revision, and what evidence permits renewed admission. This limits guidance for sustained reuse. SKILL.nb provides related context on evidence-triggered maintenance and validated updates.
Questions For Authors:
All questions are clarifying ones.

What are the complete references for the benchmarks used? Section 4.1 does not provide much detail on them.
Which labels in the reported 92-group tables include downstream answer errors? Please identify the labeling path, or specify the narrower loss and guarantee these tables support after considering the continuation limitation in Section 7.
Can the retained discovery and split records account for every record excluded by successful-trace or family-window filtering, including whether the deployed guard would reject it? This would resolve the population concern without a new benchmark study.
Beyond manifest invalidation and preset expiry, what operational evidence triggers recompilation or retirement, and what fresh calibration is required before reactivation? Please distinguish implemented policy from future work, especially for distribution changes that pass existing guards.
The notion of ``content-addressed execution manifest''
 is introduced in Section 2, without any further nor proper definition. Explain what it is, and why it matters to the episode definition.
