Response to Reviewer 2 (1/2)

Thank you for an assessment that credits the transparent reporting and names exactly what the paper had not shown; we answer your central point first.

Section, table and appendix numbers refer to the revised PDF; the September 24 version you reviewed did not contain the studies cited below.

1. Practical value (your W1 and Q3). Paraphrase: the primary tasks are metadata classification and extraction, easily handled by predefined workflows, and the handwritten comparator matches GAC. The value of automatic discovery is unquantified, and the cross-repository work keeps the simpler PR-outcome task. Q3 asks for full GAC versus unguarded replay versus a handwritten baseline on a workflow with genuinely adaptive decisions.

Answer: We agree with the facts and have changed what the paper claims. The primary families are metadata classification and verbatim extraction, and no decision in them needs semantic judgment; §4 now says so. The handwritten program matches them: 90/90 contracts and equal requests (Table 14).

We now state the contribution as safe automation of that program. GAC recovers the program an engineer would write, from traces, without being told the workflow. It refuses where it cannot justify one. Matching the handwritten program is therefore the intended result, not a shortfall.

Evidence that the automation is safe at scale (end-to-end study, Appendix F.2, Table 19):
- Eleven repositories, 3,560 fresh groups, one pull request per author, sampled before any outcome filter.
- The compiled, unchanged and handwritten arms meet 3,510, 3,507 and 3,504 contracts. The compiled and handwritten arms both cut cost by 73.6%.
- The guard refuses where evidence is insufficient: node and cpython are retired at n = 400.
- Adverse: on godot the compiled arm trails the unchanged agent, 393 vs 399 (p = 0.070).

Measured discovery cost breaks even after 411, 183 and 181 episodes per family. As you note, PR-outcome is used for the cross-repository and post-cutoff studies because it is the family with enough fresh records. Those studies test transfer and certification, not task difficulty.

What remains open:
- The engineering value of automation over hand-writing (engineer time) is not quantified.
- No adaptive-task advantage is claimed. Your Q3 experiment was not completed and cannot be before November 18; we estimate 12 to 16 working days and a budget increase.
- Three attempts stopped at prespecified development gates (Appendix L):
  - BIRD: a guarded one-call candidate answered 26/36 against 28/36 for both the unchanged agent and an exact handwritten prefetch.
  - AppWorld: the adapted baselines scored 1/12 and 2/12, below an 8/12 competence gate, so no GAC arm ran.
  - AgentHop: the baseline passed (19/24 against a 17/24 gate), but no grounded selector for the dependent read could be derived.
- The protocol is committed in the supplement (adaptive-quality-cost-study.md).

What the paper does add beyond metadata: BIRD (§5.2), a live SQL agent after a compiled schema-first opening. The opening compiles on 26/31 databases. On 150 sealed questions the compiled agent answers 97 and the unchanged agent 94, with 44.9% fewer requests. On a second model and on the training split the compiled agent trails warm reruns of the unchanged agent (93 vs 95 of 150; 402 vs 412 of 630). BIRD therefore shows savings at similar accuracy, not an accuracy gain.

Change to the paper: the abstract, §1 and the conclusion carry the safe-automation framing. The "matches handwritten" claim is limited to GitHub, because on BIRD the guarded candidate trailed the handwritten prefetch. A new table, "What the compiler decided without human input" (Table 1), opens §5.

Response to Reviewer 2 (2/2)

2. Contribution of the guards and the learned gate (your W2). Paraphrase: barrier-free replay ties GAC; disabling several barriers at once prevents attribution; the learned gate shows no ranking value; the drift comparison was not executed.

Answer: Partly agreed. The missing studies now exist, and the learned-gate claim is withdrawn.
(a) Recurrence-only replay still ties GAC at 30/30 with one fewer request on that cohort (Table 33). We do not count a refusal as an avoided error.
(b) A live-recovery study removes one mechanism at a time over 2,304 scheduled episodes (Appendix I). The scope guard raises success from 186/216 to 205/216 (+8.80 points, descriptive 95% interval [3.70, 13.89]). The completeness check's benefit is unresolved (204/216 versus 205/216).
(c) Drift was run three ways. The provider-free (144 paired groups) and recorded-replay (89 records) studies are null, with zero wrong answers in every arm. Continuation-graded drift is adverse: 39/60 wrong in both arms. The verifier bought abstention only.
(d) Full statistical, support-only and deterministic routing are identical on all 2,724 record-model cases (Table 38), so we no longer claim learned selectivity.

Change: §5.4 "Mechanism evidence"; §3 Stage 6 states that the score is implemented, every admitted operating point is full coverage, and graded selection is not claimed; Appendices G and I.

Still open: the completeness check's benefit and protection under natural drift are not established.

3. How calibration labels are generated, and filtering before the split (your Q2).

Answer:
- Primary families: by replaying existing trajectories. grc/compile.py::_calibration_samples runs the frozen program over each calibration group's recorded tool results. After verifier success it compares outputs with the recorded ones; the continuation never runs.
- Filtering: discovery runs were filtered on exact traces before the 16/8/92 split, removing two issue-type runs and one PR run, so calibration sampling followed filtering. All three excluded records pass the saved guard and gate, and the unfiltered-population claim is withdrawn (Appendix G "Calibration population audit", 396/396 records).
- End-to-end studies: two later studies execute the frozen prefix followed by the model continuation and grade the final contract. They are 132 post-cutoff PR groups (0 violations, U = 0.0173) and the multi-repository study, which samples before any outcome or recurrence filter (registered stage: 3 violations in 760 groups, U = 0.0133).

Change: the §2 loss definition; Appendix C "Calibration labels as computed"; Appendix G; the new Appendix F.2. The primary replay bounds remain on the filtered population, and the paper says so.

4. Primary-task specification (your Q1).

Answer: each sample is one issue or PR number from the pinned huggingface/datasets snapshot. The agent makes read-only calls and returns exact, source-grounded fields.
- Issue type: record, labels and comments. It returns the title, state, a label-derived category and a verbatim excerpt.
- PR outcome: the PR record, merge status and discussion. It returns open, merged or closed_unmerged, plus the title and a comment excerpt.
- Backlog attention: the open-issue record, assignees and discussion. It returns owned, discussed_unowned or awaiting_first_response, plus the title, owner and an excerpt.

No decision requires semantic interpretation or adaptive branching. The model's remaining role is reading the returned evidence and writing the contract fields.

Change: Table 16 and Appendix E "Primary workflow families".
