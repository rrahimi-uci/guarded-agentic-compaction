Response to Reviewer 2

Thank you for a review that names what the paper had not shown. Numbers refer to the revised PDF; the September 24 version lacked the studies below.

W1 (practical value: metadata tasks, a handwritten workflow matches GAC, cross-repository work keeps PR-outcome). We agree with each fact and changed the claim rather than dispute them. Section 4 now says no primary decision needs semantic judgment. The handwritten program matches GAC on contracts, 90/90 to 90/90 (Table 2), and does somewhat better on tokens and cost (Table 14). The paper now states the contribution as safe automation of that program, found from traces with refusal where it cannot be justified; matching the handwritten program is the intended result. At scale (new Appendix F.2, Table 19): eleven repositories, 3,560 fresh pull-request groups, one per author, sampled before any outcome filter; compiled, unchanged and handwritten arms meet 3,510, 3,507 and 3,504 contracts, both programs cut cost 73.6%, node and cpython are retired at n = 400. Adverse: godot 393 vs 399 (p = 0.070). What the automatic decisions are worth: a four-policy funnel over all 13 retained cohorts (Appendix N) shows statistical admission never changed a compile-time decision and grounding changed one; a sealed pilot on 193 fresh issues found that refusal did not recur, and GAC, the unguarded replay of the same program and the unchanged agent met the contract on 65/65, 63/65 and 64/65. The value we can show is reproducible, inspectable refusal, not a measured quality gain. Discovery breaks even after 411, 183 and 181 episodes (Section 5.1). Beyond metadata: BIRD text-to-SQL (Section 5.2), a live SQL agent after a compiled schema-first opening: 97 vs 94 on 150 sealed questions with 44.9% fewer requests than a warm rerun; it trails on a second model and the training split (93 vs 95; 402 vs 412). Savings at similar accuracy. Still open: engineer time saved is not quantified.

Suggested validation (GAC vs unguarded replay vs handwritten on an adaptive workflow). Not completed, and not possible before November 18; no claim depends on it. Three attempts stopped at prespecified gates (Appendix L): BIRD one-call candidate 26/36 vs 28/36 for both comparators; AppWorld baselines 1/12 and 2/12, below the 8/12 gate; AgentHop baseline passed (19/24) but no grounded selector could be derived. We estimate 12 to 16 working days and are not promising it.

W2 (guards and learned gate unestablished; drift not run). Partly agreed; the learned-gate claim is withdrawn and the missing studies now exist, with mixed results. Barrier-free replay still ties GAC at 30/30 with one fewer request (Table 33), and the funnel and pilot above agree. We do not count a refusal as an avoided error. One mechanism at a time (Appendix I, 2,304 episodes): the scope guard raises success from 186/216 to 205/216 (+8.80 points, 95% interval [3.70, 13.89]); the completeness check is unresolved (210/216 and 204/216 without it vs 205/216 with it). Drift, three ways (Section 5.4; Appendix G): a provider-free perturbation study (144 paired groups) and a recorded-replay study (89 records) are null; the continuation-graded study is adverse, 39/60 wrong in both arms; the verifier bought abstention only. Learned gate: the reviewed Table 23 comparison also changed the risk budget and grid, so it attributes nothing to the gate; full statistical, support-only and deterministic routing are identical on all 2,724 record-model cases (Section 5.4; Appendix K). Section 3, Stage 6, presents the score as implemented and graded selection as not claimed.

Calibration evidence (how labels are made; does sampling precede filtering?). Primary families: grc/compile.py::_calibration_samples replays the frozen program over recorded tool results and compares outputs after the verifier accepts; the continuation never runs (Appendix C). Discovery runs were filtered on exact traces before the 16/8/92 split (two issue-type and one PR run removed), so sampling followed filtering; all three excluded records pass the saved guard and gate, so the primary bounds do not cover the unfiltered population, and the paper withdraws that claim (Section 4; Appendix G, 396 of 396 records). The post-cutoff study (0/132, 90% bound 0.0173) and the multi-repository study (3/760, U = 0.0133) execute the continuation and grade the final contract; the second samples one pull request per author before any filter.

Task requirements. None of the decisions needs semantic or adaptive reasoning; Table 16 and Appendix E give the specification. Each sample is an issue or PR number; the agent makes read-only calls and returns exact, source-grounded fields (a label-derived or status-derived class, title, and a verbatim excerpt; owner for backlog).

We are glad to answer anything further.
