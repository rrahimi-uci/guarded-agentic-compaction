Weakness 2, first part: no comparison with other workflow compilers (EvoC2F, Agent JIT) on the cost-quality trade-off.

Answer: we did not run one, and the paper makes no ranking claim against them. This is a limitation we cannot close before the discussion ends.

- EvoC2F has no inspectable implementation (its anonymous code endpoint returned HTTP 401), and Agent JIT plans web tasks; our SQL adaptation of it is only a development probe. A matched comparison is designed (paper/supplementary/matched-compiler-candidate-protocol.md) but not run; we estimate 6 to 10 working days.
- What we did instead. (a) A pre-registered comparison with adapted offline Agent Workflow Memory: 8,172 episodes on 1,362 fresh records with two models, the six comparison cells using 1,344 records. GAC meets a five-point accuracy noninferiority margin in 5 of 6 workflow-model cells, at 36.9 to 79.1% lower estimated cost (Section 5.1, Table 3; Appendix K). (b) A comparison of design properties as each paper states them (Section 6; Appendix N, Table 46). TraceCompiler, PreAct, EvoC2F, Agent JIT, plan caching and AWM admit artifacts by support floors, test-pass thresholds, condition checks, key matches or one re-run; none states a finite-sample bound on the violation rate after admission. We also list what those systems do that GAC does not: writes, branches and loops, steps beyond the opening, online adaptation and latency scheduling.

Response to Reviewer 1 (3/3)

Weakness 5 and Question 4: the lifecycle policy is underspecified. What evidence triggers recompilation or retirement, what calibration is needed before reactivation, and which parts are implemented?

Answer: the implemented controls are manifest checks, expiry, retirement, rollback, a kill switch and staged promotion. No trigger reacts to accumulating failures or to a distribution change that passes the guards, and the paper now says so.

- Implemented: any incompatible manifest (a change to a pinned model, prompt, tool, policy, effect catalog or snapshot) is rejected at dispatch; presets expire; artifacts can be retired, rolled back or switched off.
- Not implemented: no failure-rate trigger, no sequential confidence guarantee, no measured repair benefit.
- Proposed, not evaluated: suspend on a confirmed committed contract violation or an invalidated pin; audit task outcomes and fallback rates against a prospectively specified monitoring rule; reactivate only as a new version with a declared target population, a frozen candidate and loss, and fresh disjoint calibration that includes failed baseline runs.
- Measured case: on a new snapshot the retained PR-outcome artifact abstained on all 60 fresh test pull requests; a re-derived version was admitted only after fresh calibration on 132 groups (post-cutoff study, Appendix G).
- Paper change: Appendix D, "Implemented lifecycle controls and proposed renewal policy", which cites SKILL.nb; one sentence in Section 7.

Question 1: complete references for the benchmarks.

Answer: the GitHub families use the pinned public snapshot helmo/github-issues, revision e344be7b (Table 5); BIRD, Li et al. 2023, release dev_20240627; NESTFUL, Basu et al. 2025; AppWorld gold solutions and released agent runs, Trivedi et al. 2024; API-Bank, Li et al. 2023; BFCL v4, Patil et al. 2025. AgentHop (Park et al. 2026) is used only for development gates (Appendix L). Section 4 cites each benchmark it uses; Table 16 specifies the three GitHub tasks, and Appendix F.4 maps each external benchmark to its use. In the main results no model runs on NESTFUL, AppWorld, API-Bank or BFCL v4; they are compiler substrates.

Question 5: what is a "content-addressed execution manifest", and why does it matter for the episode definition?

Answer: it is the record of which prompt, model, tools, policies and effect catalog an episode ran under, each identified by version or content digest. Section 2 now says: "The manifest identifies the prompt, model, tools, policies, and effect catalog by version or content digest; its compatibility pins define which execution environment an artifact may replace." It matters because an episode counts as evidence for an artifact only under a compatible manifest, and dispatch requires equality on every pinned field (Eq. 3), so a certificate does not transfer to a changed model, prompt, tool or snapshot. The 60/60 abstentions above are this rule working.

Summary of the changes: (1) loss, labels and claims are aligned, with replay certificates for the primary families and end-to-end certificates from two new studies; (2) a calibration-population audit was added and the unfiltered-population claim withdrawn; (3) an unfiltered, author-unit end-to-end study ran across eleven repositories; (4) an adapted AWM comparison and a design comparison with other compilers were added, with no ranking claim against EvoC2F or Agent JIT; (5) the introduction, task definitions, manifest definition and lifecycle policy were rewritten.
