General response to all reviewers

Thank you for three careful reviews of the September 24 submission. Since then we have run every study we could complete before the deadline, pre-registered, reported whatever it showed, and revised the paper; the PDF diff shows every change. The method and the primary results (now Table 2) are unchanged. Each reply answers its review; this covers the shared points.

What GAC is for. When a read-only workflow opening recurs, an engineer can hand-write a program for it. GAC automates writing that program under explicit guards: it compiles only grounded, read-only, first-step prefixes; it admits a program only under a finite-sample bound on its violation rate; and it refuses everywhere else. Matching the hand-written program is the intended result, not a shortfall.

Shared concerns

1. Replay labels versus end-to-end guarantees (R1, R2). The 92-group labels come from replaying the compiled program over recorded tool results, not executed continuations; the paper calls them replay certificates (Section 2; Appendix C). Two new studies grade the final contract after the compiled prefix and the continuation: a post-cutoff PR-outcome cohort (0 of 132 groups failed; 90% bound 0.0173) and the multi-repository study in point 3.

2. Outcome-filtered calibration population (R1, R2). An audit accounts for all 396 discovery records. The three excluded records pass the deployed guard (Appendix G), so we withdraw the unfiltered-population claim for the primary bounds. The repair was run for PR-outcome: one pull request per author, sampled before any outcome filter, every eligible group labelled, failures included (Appendix F.2, Table 19).

3. Independent units and a selection-wide bound (R2, R3). Two of the three primary families calibrated two candidates, so their selection-adjusted bound is 0.0569 and misses 5%; counting creation days or authors as the unit retires all three (Section 7; Table 31); the abstract and conclusion say so. One frozen candidate per population, authors as units, gives selection-wide end-to-end bounds for PR-outcome:
- Registered stage: four repositories, 3 violations in 760 groups, each repository admitted, pooled U = 0.0133.
- Amendment 3: seven repositories at n = 400, registered after the first four results were seen and selected by design features only. Five admitted; node and cpython retired.
- All eleven: compiled, unchanged and hand-written arms meet 3,510, 3,507 and 3,504 of 3,560 contracts; compiled and hand-written both cut cost 73.6%.
- Adverse: on godot the compiled arm trails the unchanged agent, 393 vs 399 (p = 0.070). Post hoc: 47 of 50 compiled violations are non-verbatim excerpts; none is a wrong merge outcome.

4. Learned gate (R2, R3). We no longer claim learned selectivity: full statistical, support-only and deterministic routing are identical on all 2,724 record-model cases (Section 5.4; Appendix K), and 92-group cohorts certify only full coverage.

5. Practical value against alternatives (R1, R2). New: a pre-registered comparison with adapted offline Agent Workflow Memory, 8,172 episodes on 1,362 fresh records; five of the six cells (1,344 records) are noninferior at a five-point margin, at 36.9 to 79.1% lower cost (Table 3). New: BIRD text-to-SQL with a live agent; 97 vs 94 correct on 150 sealed questions with 44.9% fewer requests than a warm rerun, trailing warm reruns on a second model and the training split (93 vs 95; 402 vs 412): savings at similar accuracy (Section 5.2). New: a design comparison with six trace-reuse systems (Appendix N); none states a finite-sample bound after admission. New (Appendix N): a same-IR compiler funnel (statistical admission on/off, grounding on/off) over all 13 retained cohorts and a sealed live pilot on 193 fresh issues: admission never changed a decision; grounding changed one, which did not recur on fresh data; live, GAC 65/65, the unguarded replay of the same program 63/65, the unchanged agent 64/65 at four requests. New: a one-mechanism-at-a-time live-recovery study (2,304 episodes; Appendix I); three drift studies.

Not done, and no claim depends on them: a comparison with an executable external compiler (none has runnable code; the same-IR funnel is the matched comparison we could run); GAC versus unguarded replay versus a handwritten program on an adaptive workflow (three attempts stopped at development gates, Appendix L); a learned-versus-support gate comparison at two certified coverage levels, which needs a cohort we do not have. We claim no advantage on adaptive tasks, no benefit from the learned gate, and no ranking against other compilers.

Paper changes, in brief: abstract, introduction and conclusion rewritten around safe automation and its limits; Section 2 aligns the loss with the labels; Section 5.4 and Appendix F.2 report the end-to-end studies; Appendix G the population audit; Appendices I to L the mechanism, comparison and adaptive-gate studies; Appendix N the compiler comparisons.
