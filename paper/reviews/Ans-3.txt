Response to Reviewer 3

Thank you for the precise statistical reading; both concerns changed the paper's claims. Numbers refer to the revised PDF.

W1 and Q1 (safety language exceeds the statistics; does any cohort support a selection-wide 5% bound?). You are right about the primary families, and the abstract and conclusion now say so. Zero violations in 92 groups give U = 0.0498 for one fixed candidate (Table 6). Issue-type routing calibrated one candidate, so its replay bound is selection-wide; PR-outcome and backlog routing calibrated two, so their adjusted bound is 0.0569 (a fixed two-candidate design needs 106 groups); counting creation days or authors as the unit retires all three (Table 31).

For PR-outcome, two new studies with one frozen candidate give selection-wide bounds below 5% that grade final answers, not replay. Post-cutoff cohort (Section 5.4; Table 28; Appendix G): one candidate frozen before calibration (m = 1), 0 end-to-end violations in 132 pull requests opened after the snapshot, U = 0.0173 under the single pre-registered 90% rule, 0.035 under the registered grid, 0.0267 with its 85 distinct authors as units. Multi-repository study (new Appendix F.2, Table 19): one pull request per author, sampled before any outcome filter, every eligible group labelled, failures included, one candidate frozen per repository, admission rule fixed before any call. Registered stage, all admitted: datasets 1/200 (U = 0.0333), pandas 2/200 (0.0419), streamlit 0/160 (0.0290), pytorch 0/200 (0.0232); pooled 3/760, U = 0.0133; psf/requests not run (99 author-distinct groups, below the 106 minimum). Amendment 3 added seven repositories at n = 400 (grid admits at most 9 violations); registered after the first four results were seen, selecting by design features only. Admitted: next.js (1), django (2), kubernetes (3), vscode (3), godot (7); retired: node (14; U = 0.0632), cpython (17; 0.0726), the rule refusing as designed. Over all eleven the compiled, unchanged and hand-written arms meet 3,510, 3,507 and 3,504 of 3,560 contracts; the pooled row (k = 50, U = 0.0194) assumes exchangeable repositories, which we do not claim. Adverse: godot 393 vs 399 (McNemar p = 0.070); post hoc, 47 of 50 compiled violations are non-verbatim excerpts and none a wrong merge outcome. pytorch has no merged class (its merge bot does not set merged_at); without it the registered pool is 3/560, U = 0.0181. Each end-to-end bound is for one family on one repository and assumes independent authors, a design assumption, not a result. The abstract and conclusion state that two primary families miss the selection-adjusted target and that end-to-end bounds cover one family; Section 7 states the day and author sensitivity.

W2 and Q2 (learned gate; compare with a support-based gate at two or more positive certified coverage levels). Agreed; the paper no longer claims the learned gate ranks safer cases higher. We did not run the matched-coverage comparison: no cohort we hold is large enough, and it cannot be run before the discussion closes. With zero violations, certifying coverage c needs at least ceil(92/c) groups, so the 92-group cohorts certify only full coverage or retirement (Figure 3). The gate score is constant within a repository whenever the entry state is a record number; full statistical, support-only and deterministic routing are identical on all 2,724 record-model cases of the AWM comparison (Section 5.4; Appendix K), and at compile time support-only admission (alpha = 1) admits exactly what full admission admits on all 13 retained cohorts (Appendix N). The one larger cohort, a sequential extension of the issue gate (Appendix H.1, Table 37), selects eta = 0.50 on 311 pooled groups: 261/311 dispatched, 4 errors, nominal U = 0.0443; it was decided after a retirement and mixes sampling frames, so it is not a certificate, and at eta = 0.02 its errors split 4/194 above vs 0/117 at or below the threshold (one-sided Fisher p = 0.150): no evidence of graded selectivity. Its sealed test at unmatched coverage: learned routing dispatched 76/90, support-only accepts all 90; errors 3/90 and 5/90 vs 2/90 unchanged. Suggestive only (paper/results/graded_issue_gate/audit_extension.json). Feasibility: a provider-free screen of a single-look replication on the untouched post-cutoff issue pool recorded a no-go (paper/results/graded_issue_gate_tf/preflight.json): 277 issues, 197 with a comment; after 80 for re-derivation at most 117 calibration groups remain, 31 to 113 under the exclusion rules, against about 150 needed to admit one grid point; two positive coverage levels need more. Section 3, Stage 6, presents the score as implemented and the admission test as a support floor; the abstract and conclusion state that we claim no benefit from a learned gate.
