Response to Reviewer 3 (2/2)

W2 and Q2: the evidence does not show that the learned gate scores safer cases higher, and at 92 groups only zero or full coverage can be certified. Could you compare the learned gate with a support-based gate on one held-out population at two or more positive, certified coverage levels?

Answer: agreed, and the paper no longer claims that the learned gate ranks safer cases higher. We did not run the matched-coverage comparison, because no cohort we hold is large enough, and we cannot run it before the discussion closes.

Evidence:
- With zero violations, certifying coverage c needs at least ceil(92/c) groups, so the 92-group cohorts certify only full coverage or retirement (Figure 3).
- The gate score is constant within a repository whenever the entry state is a record number. Full statistical, support-only and deterministic routing are identical on all 2,724 record-model cases of the AWM comparison (Section 5.4; Appendix K).
- The one larger cohort, a sequential extension of the issue gate (Appendix H.1, Table 37), selects eta = 0.50 on 311 pooled groups: 261/311 dispatched, 4 errors, nominal U = 0.0443. The extension was decided after a retirement and mixes sampling frames, so it is not a certificate. At eta = 0.02 its errors split 4/194 above versus 0/117 at or below the threshold (one-sided Fisher p = 0.150): no evidence of graded selectivity.
- Its sealed test compares the two gates at unmatched coverage: learned routing dispatched 76/90 issues, and the support-only gate accepts all 90. Errors were 3/90 and 5/90, against 2/90 for the unchanged agent. Suggestive only.
- Source: paper/results/graded_issue_gate/audit_extension.json.

Feasibility: we screened a single-look replication on the untouched post-cutoff issue pool and recorded it as a no-go. The post-cutoff snapshot holds 277 issues, 197 with at least one comment; after 80 for candidate re-derivation at most 117 remain, which cannot support two positive certified coverage levels (each needs at least ceil(92/c) groups). A valid test needs a larger fresh cohort with an informative entry state and a new task specification. The prospective protocol is in Appendix H.

Paper change: Section 3, Stage 6, presents the score as implemented, not claimed, and the admission test as a support floor. The abstract and conclusion state that we claim no benefit from a learned dispatch gate.

Summary of the changes: (1) the abstract and conclusion state that the primary bounds cover replay on selected populations and that two primary families miss the selection-adjusted 5% target; Section 7 states the creation-day and author sensitivity; (2) two end-to-end studies with one frozen candidate per population were added: 0/132 (U = 0.0173) and the author-unit multi-repository study, which admits 9 of the 11 repositories run and retires 2; (3) the learned-gate claim is withdrawn.

We are glad to answer anything further during the discussion period.
