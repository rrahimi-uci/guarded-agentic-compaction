Tool-using agents repeat mature read-only evidence paths while paying for a model at
every boundary. Deterministic programs can reduce cost, but recurrence is unsafe:
arguments may lack provenance,
tools may cross effect or permission boundaries, and a replay-correct prefix may still
change the final answer.

We introduce \emph{guarded agentic compaction} (\method), a trace-to-program compiler that
reconstructs typed value provenance, rejects unsafe effects, synthesizes from a closed
23-operator language, verifies empirical contracts, and applies per-candidate exact
finite-sample threshold admission. Its normal output is refusal: compile only a supported
prefix; otherwise retain the unchanged agent. Guarded composite synthesis exposes an admitted program
before the first model request without discarding its internal checks.

We evaluate three distinct GitHub workflows with live provider calls. On 90 held-out
public records, compiled programs pass 90/90 exact contracts and unchanged agents 89/90 ---
not a significant difference (exact McNemar $p=1$), though no compiled-only failure occurs
and discordance is bounded at 3.3\%. Compiled programs reduce provider requests by
50.0--75.0\%, tokens by 39.5--81.4\%, observed latency by 51.7--73.0\%, and estimated cost
by 32.0--75.3\%.
Hand-written programs also reach 90/90 and tie learned programs on the two new families, so
automatic discovery, conservative admission, and managed lifecycle---not runtime
dominance---are the contribution. On NESTFUL and API-Bank, every
recurrent family retires for insufficient support. We report each artifact's per-candidate
selective-risk level, because three are licensed only at 10\% rather than the registered
5\%; candidate-family search is not multiplicity-adjusted.

The central result is an evidence boundary: traces establish recurrence, not
admissibility. Agent workflow optimizers should be evaluated by what they safely refuse
as well as by what they accelerate.
