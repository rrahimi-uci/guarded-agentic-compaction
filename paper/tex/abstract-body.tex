Tool-using agents often repeat mature, read-only evidence paths while paying for a model
decision at every boundary. Replacing those decisions with deterministic programs can
reduce cost, but recurrence alone is unsafe: arguments may lack observable provenance,
tools may cross effect or permission boundaries, and a replay-correct prefix may still
change the final answer.

We introduce \emph{guarded agentic compaction} (\method), a trace-to-program compiler that
reconstructs typed value provenance, rejects unsafe effects, synthesizes from a closed
23-operator language, verifies empirical contracts, and applies exact finite-sample
admission. Its normal output is refusal: compile only the supported prefix, otherwise
retain the unchanged agent. Guarded composite synthesis can expose an admitted program
before the first model request without discarding its internal checks.

We evaluate three distinct GitHub workflows with live provider calls. On 90 held-out
public records, exact task outcomes move from 89/90 to 90/90. Relative to the
unchanged agents, compiled programs reduce provider requests by 50.0--75.0\%, tokens by
39.5--81.4\%, observed latency by 51.7--73.0\%, and estimated cost by 32.0--75.3\%.
Hand-written programs also reach 90/90; on the two new families they tie learned programs.
Automatic discovery, conservative admission, and managed lifecycle---rather than runtime
dominance---are the contribution. On NESTFUL and API-Bank, every
recurrent family is retired for insufficient support.

The central result is an evidence boundary: traces establish recurrence, not
admissibility. Agent workflow optimizers should be evaluated by what they safely refuse
as well as by what they accelerate.
