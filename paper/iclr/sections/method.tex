\paragraph{1. Typed provenance.}
A capture adapter normalizes framework traces into the Episode IR; manifests pin
prompt, policy, guardrail, tool, model, SDK, tracer, entry-contract, and
effect-catalog identities, and episodes with different compatibility keys are
partitioned before any learning. For each call-argument slot, the
program-argument trace graph (\patg, \cref{alg:patg}) searches entry-state and
prior-result paths for typed values reachable by a bounded transform ---
execution provenance in the database sense~\citep{cheney2009provenance},
specialized to tool arguments. Two slot marks make the region miner block rather than guess:
a slot with \emph{no} witness is a genuine model decision, and one with more
than $\kappa=3$ witnesses is ambiguous (\cref{fig:provenance}). A repeated tool
name, a matching literal, and a successful replay are not witnesses; only an
expression in $\mathcal{L}$ is.

\paragraph{2. Region mining and ranking.}
The miner enumerates model-boundary-to-result windows of at most $w_{\max}$ tool
calls, qualifies live-ins and effects against a human-signed rather than
inferred catalog, and hashes tool signatures, dependency topology, run shape,
and live-in/live-out shape while abstracting literals. Families require support
across independent scenario \emph{groups}, not raw event frequency, so one
chatty scenario cannot manufacture recurrence. Survivors are ranked by an
MDL-inspired trade-off that pays for expected savings and charges
for heterogeneity, effect exposure, and program size:
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{score}(F)=
  \underbrace{s_F\,\bar{k}_F\,c_m}_{\text{support-weighted saving}}
  -\lambda_1 \mathsf{Ent}(F)-\lambda_2\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(F)-\lambda_3|F| ,
  \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
with $s_F$ supporting train groups, $\bar{k}_F$ the mean removable model requests,
$c_m$ their unit cost, and $\mathsf{Ent}(F)$ the Shannon entropy over canonical
variants inside the family. \Cref{eq:score} affects only \emph{which} family is
tried first.

\paragraph{3. Feasibility ceiling.}
Before any synthesis runs, an estimator bounds what the compiler could achieve
with a perfect verifier and a gate that never abstains:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\max}=\frac{\varphi\,k}{n_B}\ \ge\ \Delta
  \quad\text{(pre-synthesis filter)},
  \qquad
  \varphi\,\rho\,k \ \ge\ \Delta\,n_B
  \quad\text{(realized, post-verification)},
  \label{eq:ceiling}
\end{equation}
with $n_B$ baseline model requests per episode, $\varphi$ the fraction of
episodes holding an eligible region, $k$ the requests removed per successful
dispatch, and $\rho$ the verifier pass rate. Stage~3 runs before synthesis, so
no verifier exists and it evaluates only the left form, which fixes $\rho=1$;
the right form is what the workload must meet once $V$ is induced. Since
$\rho\le 1$, no measured reduction exceeds $\Delta_{\max}$, so a workload under
target is declined before a compiler is built for it.

\paragraph{4--5. Bounded synthesis and contracts.}
Synthesis searches the 23-operator library of \cref{tab:dsl} at depth at most
two; bindings are ranked by cross-group stability, and a group-wise refit
rejects bindings exploiting row-level coincidence. The hypothesis space is
deliberately small enough to read --- the approach resembles
programming-by-example~\citep{gulwani2011flashfill} and bounded
sketching~\citep{solarlezama2006sketching} --- and it does \emph{not} generate
arbitrary Python. Hard guards then constrain entry types, required fields,
categorical sets, numeric intervals, patterns, isolation keys, allowed effects,
and manifest pins; verifiers constrain live-out presence, type, cardinality,
empirical hulls, provenance, effect multisets, and call counts. These are
candidate invariants, not proofs --- which is why a statistical gate follows
rather than replaces them.

\paragraph{6. Exact risk-gated admission.}
\Cref{alg:calibrate} decides deployment. The score $q$ is a logistic model over
entry-observable features --- missing fields, hull margin, temporal drift, and
provenance ambiguity --- fitted by regularized maximum likelihood on \emph{dev}
groups only, never on calibration groups, then frozen together with the finite
threshold grid
$\Lambda=\{0.02,0.05,0.08,0.11,0.14,0.17,0.20,0.25,0.30,0.40,0.50\}$. Two data signals do different jobs. $q$ is \emph{fitted} to predict an
\emph{unproductive} dev group --- guard rejection, abstention, or manifest
mismatch --- never a wrong execution, since \textsc{ReplayAndChallenge} already
retired those; the bound below is \emph{counted} only on dispatched groups that
turned out wrong (\cref{app:config}). For each
$\eta\in\Lambda$ the compiler counts admitted calibration groups ($n_\eta$) and
those containing at least one post-dispatch violation ($k_\eta$), and inverts
the one-sided exact binomial test~\citep{clopper1934binomial} at a
Bonferroni-corrected level, with explicit empty and all-violation cases:
\begin{equation}
  U_\eta=\begin{cases}
  1, & n_\eta=0\ \text{or}\ k_\eta=n_\eta,\\
  \operatorname{Beta}^{-1}\!\left(1-\tfrac{\delta}{|\Lambda|};
  k_\eta+1,n_\eta-k_\eta\right), & 0\le k_\eta<n_\eta,
  \end{cases}
  \quad U_\eta\big|_{k_\eta=0}=1-\left(\tfrac{\delta}{|\Lambda|}\right)^{1/n_\eta}.
  \label{eq:cp}
\end{equation}
It admits the largest-coverage threshold with $U_\eta\le\alpha$ and retires
otherwise --- a fixed-grid instance of learn-then-test~\citep{angelopoulos2022ltt}. The closed form on the right is the operative
constraint here: at $\alpha=0.05$, $\delta=0.1$, $|\Lambda|=11$, a zero-violation family still needs $n_\eta\ge 92$ admitted groups, and the default
output is refusal.

That floor also bounds what selectivity is \emph{certifiable}: coverage
$c=n_\eta/|\mathcal{K}|$ needs $|\mathcal{K}|\ge\lceil 92/c\rceil$, so on the
$|\mathcal{K}|=92$ cohorts used throughout --- the smallest satisfiable pool ---
the only admissible coverages are $c\in\{0,1\}$ whatever $q$ ranks, since
abstaining on one group leaves $U_\eta=0.0503>\alpha$. Intermediate coverage can
still be \emph{observed}; it cannot be certified (\cref{app:config}). These
studies therefore test support sufficiency at full coverage and cannot identify
whether $q$ ranks dispatch risk.

\begin{proposition}[Per-candidate selective-risk admission]
\label{prop:admission}
Condition on train/development data and fix one candidate $(P,H,V,q)$,
group-level violation rule, and finite grid $\Lambda$ before observing
calibration. If calibration groups are i.i.d.\ from the future-group
distribution, then with probability at least $1-\delta$, every
$\eta\in\Lambda$ satisfies $r_\eta\le U_\eta$, where
$r_\eta$ is $\Pr(W^\eta=1\mid D^\eta=1)$ when population admission is positive
and is defined as zero otherwise. Consequently the selected $\hat\eta$ satisfies
$r_{\hat\eta}\le\alpha$ whenever $U_{\hat\eta}\le\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\Cref{app:proof} gives the proof. The guarantee is per fixed candidate and
conditional on i.i.d.\ groups, which the cohorts assume rather than establish
(\cref{sec:discussion}). Conditional on train and development data the number
$m$ of candidates reaching calibration is fixed, so a run is compiler-wide when
$m=1$ (\cref{cor:frozen}) or when $\gamma$ is divided by $m$; \cref{app:config}
reports $m$ and both bounds for every artifact. Guarded deployment requires an
explicit evidence boundary, not just a recurrent trace and a successful replay.
