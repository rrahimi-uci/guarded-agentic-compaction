\section{Where the Compiler's Decisions Come From: Census, Funnel and a Live Pilot}
\label{app:expansion-evidence}

This appendix reports three pre-registered studies run after the ICLR 2027 reviews. None adds a
claim to the paper; together they say where the prefix compiler's scope ends, which of its two
admission mechanisms decide anything on the retained workloads, and what one such decision costs
when measured live.

\paragraph{Interior headroom (census).}
Over released trajectories of three agents we counted read-only regions of at least two calls
that start \emph{after} the prefix frontier and whose arguments ground to the entry state or, at
the region's boundary, to a prior result (Table~\ref{tab:interior-census}). The registered
criterion (share $\ge 20\%$ and one family supported by $\ge 92$ independent groups) passes on
OpenHands and $\tau$-bench and fails on SWE-agent. The $\tau$-bench pass is one pattern
(``fetch every record the previous lookup listed'', support 96 tasks against a floor of 92) that
needs boundary-local projection of a prior result; the OpenHands pass is 37.5\% attributable to
a model-side \texttt{think} call that ends the registered prefix at call~0, and the remainder is
the opening the prefix census already described. Interior reuse therefore has a target on
structured-API agents and not, with the current grounding rule, on coding agents whose
observations are text.

\paragraph{Which mechanism decides (funnel).}
We ran the shipped compiler four times over every retained GitHub discovery cohort with two
switches: statistical admission on or off ($\alpha = 0.05$ or $1$) and grounding on or off (an
ungroundable slot refused, or bound to its modal training literal). Splits, manifests and
catalogs are identical; the full policy reproduces every retained decision
(Table~\ref{tab:compiler-funnel}). Support-only admits exactly what \method{} admits on all
thirteen cohorts, the compile-time counterpart of the runtime null of
Appendix~\ref{app:generalization-gate}. Grounding changed one decision: it refused the issue-type
three-read candidate whose \texttt{limit} had no witness (15 of 16 training windows used 100).
The grounding-off policies admit it with \texttt{limit = 100}; replayed on the 92 calibration
groups it has zero violations and one unproductive sample. On this cohort the refusal cost one
request per episode and prevented no replay-visible error (compare Table~\ref{tab:recurrence-only}).

\paragraph{The divergence, live, on fresh issues (pilot).}
Under a sealed protocol we re-ran the issue-type family on 193 post-cutoff issues: 128 discovery
episodes by the unchanged agent, a fresh compile with the retained configuration, and three arms
paired on the disjoint 65 test issues (Table~\ref{tab:compiler-funnel-pilot}). All 16 training
traces used \texttt{limit = 100}, so \method{} itself admitted the three-read program and all
four policies agreed: the retained refusal was split-dependent, not a property of the workload.
Live, the guarded program met the contract on 65/65 issues at one request each; the unguarded
replay of the identical program on 63/65; the unchanged agent on 64/65 at four requests. Every
miss is an excerpt paraphrase with all other fields exact; none is a wrong read, and the two
compiled arms differ in how evidence reaches the model, so the 0 versus 2 contrast (exact
McNemar $p = 0.50$) is not attributable to the guards. Provider spend was \$0.23.

\paragraph{What these sample sizes can see.}
Table~\ref{tab:expansion-power} gives the accepted-group floors that admit $k$ violations under
the registered grid and under the single rule of the end-to-end certificates, the comparator
miss rate a paired pilot of $n$ issues detects at 80\% power when \method{}'s arm is clean, and
the forced runs of a retired candidate needed to observe one violation. The 30-record ablation
could see only a comparator missing a quarter of the time; the 65-pair pilot, about one in eight;
about 150 pairs would be needed to see 5\%. On these workloads the binding constraint is fresh
independent groups, not provider cost.

\paragraph{What is not shown.}
No external compiler was run (none has a runnable artifact); no adaptive workload was compiled;
no candidate that \method{} retires and a simpler compiler deploys was force-run live, because the
only such candidate in the retained cohorts ran clean under replay and was admitted by \method{}
on fresh data. The paper's claim stays what it was: safe automation, under explicit guards and a
finite-sample bound, of the read-only program a person would write by hand.
