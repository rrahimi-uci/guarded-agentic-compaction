Across the 90 held-out records, \method satisfies 90/90 exact contracts and the
unchanged agent 89/90 (\cref{tab:primary}, \cref{fig:results} left), reducing provider requests by
66.6\%, tokens by 63.1\%, latency by 64.2\%, and cost by 58.7\% (per-record
values and bootstrap intervals in \cref{tab:absolute-resources,tab:reduction-ci}, \cref{app:stats}); issue-type routing, a
two-read prefix, is the low end of every range in \cref{fig:results}. These are
marginal per-episode savings within one snapshot: every artifact pins its
snapshot digest and abstains on any other (a new snapshot is recompiled), and
discovery cost alone breaks even after 411, 183, and 181 episodes per family
(\cref{app:stats}).

The discordant pair is one baseline-only miss (backlog record 5189, an
ungrounded excerpt) against zero compiled-only misses (McNemar $p=1$; one-sided
95\% bound on compiled-only discordance 3.3\%); a rerun of the 30 records scored
30/30 in every arm (\cref{app:limits}), so no accuracy difference is detected; equivalence and noninferiority were
not established. Programs hand-written with full workflow knowledge also reach
90/90, tie on requests, use fewer interfaces and tokens, cost marginally less,
and lose only on latency (\cref{tab:manual-resources}): we claim discovery and
admission, not runtime dominance.

Each artifact was admitted at 92/92 zero-violation calibration groups
($U_{\hat\eta}=0.0498\le0.05$, conditional on i.i.d.\ groups), a
support-sufficiency certificate (\cref{sec:method}) that is compiler-wide for
issue-type routing (one candidate at calibration) and per-candidate for
PR-outcome and backlog routing (two; corrected $U=0.0569$, so compiler-wide
only at $\alpha\ge0.057$; \cref{cor:frozen}, \cref{app:config}). The one certificate whose event includes the continuation comes from a
post-cutoff PR-outcome cohort (pull requests created after the snapshot's last
day; \cref{app:limits}): the re-derived artifact
dispatched on 132 fresh calibration groups and the graded task contract missed
0, a one-sided bound of 0.0173 under the single rule pre-registered for that
study and 0.035 under the primary grid. Those groups are 84 open and 48 merged
pull requests and no closed-unmerged ones, so the certified population excludes
that class; the 60 fresh test records (20 per class) pass under every
condition, and hypothetical bounds using days and authors as independent units are
0.0228 and 0.0267; independence of those clusters is unverified.

On \code{gpt-6-luna} and Anthropic \code{claude-sonnet-5}, re-discovery from
each model's own traces on the same cohorts re-derives the identical programs
on two of the three families and refuses backlog routing (on Sonnet at 91 and
90 of 92 groups, the floor binding), with four compiled-only excerpt misses on
Sonnet; transferring the retained artifacts to \code{gpt-6-luna} keeps the
structure with one compiled-only miss in 90 (\cref{app:limits}).

\subsection{Newer records within a snapshot: compile or retire}
\label{sec:res-multirepo}

On the two-read cross-repository PR-outcome task (\cref{sec:setup}), \method completes 120/120 exact contracts on
paired held-out records across four of five repositories and retires
\code{pytorch/pytorch} at admission, where no calibration group is accepted at
any threshold (\cref{app:breadth}). The compiled program ran on 80/120 records:
every open-class record abstained on the induced \code{pr.state} hull
(discovery pools held 0--3 open records) and ran the unchanged agent, so
requests fall 44.4\% (66.7\% on dispatched records) and tokens 52.4\%. A
balanced three-repository rerun with all three classes in discovery,
\code{pytorch/pytorch} included, dispatches on 180/180 and reaches 180/180 at
66.7\% fewer requests and 78.4\% fewer tokens (\cref{tab:multirepo-disaggregated},
\cref{app:breadth}), consistent with a composition-driven refusal. The
hand-written program is more efficient in the core protocol and tied in the
rerun.

\subsection{A public benchmark where \method helps: BIRD}
\label{sec:res-bird}

A live SQL agent on BIRD~\citep{li2023bird}, a public text-to-SQL benchmark,
shows where \method helps (\cref{app:bird}): on the five dev databases with at least 146 questions,
\method retires all five when the agent picks its tables per question, and
admits the schema-first opening on all five. On 150 sealed questions the
compiled agent dispatched every time, answered 97 correctly against 94 (McNemar
$p=0.45$), and cut requests 44.9\% and latency 29.4\% against a warm rerun of the
unchanged agent. Retrospective all-scheduled counts are 93 against 91 of 150
on a second model, and 402 against 403 of 630 on 21 of 26 training databases.
Requests fall 49.6\% and 45.8\% on 150 and 629 completed pairs, respectively.
A conservative paired 95\% interval for the training difference is
$[-3.27,+2.96]$ points under i.i.d.\ pairs; \cref{tab:bird-quality-sensitivity}
reports database sensitivity. A second provider's agent never ran a fixed
opening and was refused (\cref{tab:bird-extensions}).

\emph{Thin evidence.} NESTFUL, the largest trace corpus the compiler ingests here
(1{,}415 traces), passes provenance, synthesis, and replay with \emph{no} wrong
execution, yet its best family reaches $n_{\max}=26<92$, so every family retires
at stage 6 (\cref{tab:funnel}, \cref{fig:results} right); API-Bank and BFCL v4
retire the same way at 8 and 15 (\cref{tab:funnel-all}).

\emph{Admissible, rarely dispatchable.} AppWorld's gold solutions reach
$n_{\max}=136$, and \method admits one argument-free two-call artifact at 92/92
zero-violation groups ($U_{\hat\eta}=0.0498$, compiler-wide under one effect
arm, per-candidate under the other; \cref{app:external}). Yet across 8{,}190
released trajectories it is entry-eligible in $37.9\%$, almost always for
full-code agents and almost never for ReAct and plan-and-execute, which open by
reading API documentation (\cref{tab:appworld-dispatch}); admitting that read as
a prologue recovers only 2 more of 4{,}680 (\cref{app:limits}).
