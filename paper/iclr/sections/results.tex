Across the 90 held-out records, \method satisfies 90/90 exact contracts and the
unchanged agent 89/90 (\cref{tab:primary}, \cref{fig:results} left), reducing provider requests by
66.6\%, tokens by 63.1\%, latency by 64.2\%, and cost by 58.7\% (per-record
values and bootstrap intervals in \cref{tab:absolute-resources,tab:reduction-ci}, \cref{app:stats}); issue-type routing, a
two-read prefix, is the low end of every range in \cref{fig:results}. These are
marginal per-episode savings within one snapshot: discovery cost alone breaks
even after 411, 182, and 181 episodes per family (\cref{app:stats}).

The discordant pair is one baseline-only miss (backlog record 5189, an
ungrounded excerpt) against zero compiled-only misses (McNemar $p=1$; one-sided
95\% bound on compiled-only discordance 3.3\%, or 2.2\% over 210 records with
the 120-record extension of \cref{app:stats} and its one compiled-only miss
pooled); a rerun of the 30 records scored 30/30 in every arm
(\cref{app:limits}), so the result supports preservation, not superiority. Each artifact was
admitted with 92 calibration groups and zero replay-contract violations
($U_{\hat\eta}=0.0498\le0.05$), conditional on i.i.d.\ groups; because that
event has no support on these cohorts (\cref{sec:method}), the decision reduces
to the 92-group floor, which is why the second-provider backlog refusal below
turns on 90 and 91 eligible groups. The certificate is compiler-wide for
issue-type routing (one candidate at calibration) and per-candidate for
PR-outcome and backlog routing (two; corrected $U=0.0569$, so compiler-wide
only at $\alpha\ge0.057$; \cref{cor:frozen}, \cref{app:config}). On \code{gpt-6-luna} and on
Anthropic \code{claude-sonnet-5}, same cohorts, re-discovery from each model's
own traces re-derives the identical programs on two of the three families and
refuses the third, with four compiled-only excerpt misses on Sonnet; the
transfer of the retained artifacts to \code{gpt-6-luna} keeps the structure
with one compiled-only miss in 90 (\cref{app:limits}).

The one certificate whose event includes the continuation comes from a
post-cutoff PR-outcome cohort (\cref{app:limits}): the re-derived artifact
dispatched on 132 fresh calibration groups and the graded task contract missed
0, a one-sided bound of 0.0173 under the single rule pre-registered for that
study and 0.035 under the primary grid. Those groups are 84 open and 48 merged
pull requests and no closed-unmerged ones, so the certified population excludes
that class; the 60 fresh test records (20 per class) pass under every
condition, and the bound survives day- and author-level units (0.0228, 0.0267).

Discovery reaches the manual ceiling: programs written with full workflow
knowledge also reach 90/90, tie on requests, issue fewer interfaces, cost
marginally less, and lose only on latency (\cref{tab:manual-resources}). We
claim discovery and admission, not runtime dominance.

\subsection{Newer records within a snapshot: compile or retire}
\label{sec:res-multirepo}

On the narrower two-read pull-request outcome task across frozen public
repositories (\cref{sec:setup}), \method completes 120/120 exact contracts on
paired held-out records across four of five repositories and retires
\code{pytorch/pytorch} at admission, where no calibration group is accepted at
any threshold (\cref{app:breadth}). The compiled program ran on 80/120 records:
every open-class record abstained on the induced \code{pr.state} hull
(discovery pools held 0--3 open records) and ran the unchanged agent, so
requests fall 44.4\% (66.7\% on dispatched records) and tokens 52.4\%. A
balanced three-repository rerun with all three classes in discovery,
\code{pytorch/pytorch} included, dispatches on 180/180 and reaches 180/180 at
66.7\% fewer requests and 78.4\% fewer tokens (\cref{tab:multirepo-disaggregated},
\cref{app:breadth}); the refusal was composition-driven. The hand-written
program is more efficient in the core protocol and tied in the rerun. Every
artifact pins its snapshot digest and abstains on any other, so savings hold
within one snapshot; a new snapshot is recompiled, as the post-cutoff study does.

The external substrates isolate the binding constraint (\cref{tab:funnel}). All
four pass provenance, synthesis, and replay with \emph{no} wrong execution
(API-Bank's two replay windows abstain), but NESTFUL, API-Bank, and executed
BFCL gold plans retire at stage 6 on support alone, $n_{\max}=26$, $8$,
$15<92$, an outcome fixed before the compiler ran. AppWorld's gold solutions
reach $n_{\max}=136$, the first external corpus where \cref{eq:cp} is
satisfiable; it admits one argument-free two-call
artifact at 92/92 zero-violation groups ($U_{\hat\eta}=0.0498$) with 11/11 exact
replay on its sealed split under both pre-registered effect arms, compiler-wide
under arm A ($m=1$) and per-candidate under arm B, where six candidates reached
calibration (corrected bound 0.068); its 92 task groups come from 46 scenarios
and 79 supervisors, units at which the bound retires (\cref{app:external}).

Admissibility is not dispatchability: across AppWorld's 28 released baseline
runs (8{,}190 trajectories) the admitted region is entry-eligible in $37.9\%$,
almost always for full-code agents and almost never for ReAct and
plan-and-execute (\cref{tab:appworld-dispatch}, \cref{app:external}), which
open by reading API documentation; admitting that read as a prologue recovers
only 2 more of 4{,}680, since they rarely execute the region (\cref{app:limits}).
The measurement bounds $\varphi$ from above.

A live SQL agent on BIRD~\citep{li2023bird} shows the other side
(\cref{app:bird}): on the five dev databases with at least 146 questions,
\method retires all five when the agent picks its tables per question, and
admits the schema-reading opening on all five when its workflow reads every
table first. On 150 sealed questions the
compiled agent dispatched every time, answered 97 correctly against 94
(McNemar $p=0.45$), and cut model requests 44.9\%, tokens 9.8\%, latency
29.4\%, and cost 6.3\% against a warm rerun of the unchanged agent.
