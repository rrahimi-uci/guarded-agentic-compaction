Across the 90 held-out records, \method satisfies 90/90 exact contracts and the
unchanged agent 89/90 (\cref{tab:primary}), reducing provider requests by
66.6\%, tokens by 63.1\%, latency by 64.2\%, and cost by 58.7\% (per-record
values and bootstrap intervals in \cref{app:stats}); issue-type routing, a
two-read prefix, is the low end of every range. These are
marginal per-episode savings within one snapshot: every artifact pins its
snapshot digest and abstains on any other, and discovery cost alone breaks
even after 411, 183, and 181 episodes per family. The discordant pair is one baseline-only miss (backlog record 5189, an
ungrounded excerpt) against zero compiled-only misses (McNemar $p=1$; one-sided
95\% bound on compiled-only discordance 3.3\%); a rerun of the 30 records scored
30/30 in every arm (\cref{app:limits}), so no accuracy difference is detected, and neither equivalence nor noninferiority is established. Programs hand-written with full workflow knowledge also reach
90/90, tie on requests, use fewer interfaces and tokens, cost marginally less,
and lose only on pooled latency (\cref{tab:manual-resources}): we claim discovery and
admission, not runtime dominance.

Against an automated reuse baseline, a separately frozen three-workflow,
two-model comparison with an adapted offline Agent Workflow
Memory~\citep{wang2025awm} attempts all 8{,}172 episodes on 1{,}362 fresh
records (\cref{tab:awm};
\cref{app:matched-reuse,app:generalization-gate}). \method uses 50--75\% fewer
requests and 36.9--79.1\% less estimated deployment cost than adapted AWM,
whose induced workflow text leaves every step a model decision. Five of six
cells pass the registered five-point accuracy noninferiority margin with
six-comparison correction; second-model backlog is inconclusive (142/144 versus
143/144; loss bound 5.86\%). Only the two PR cells pass a two-point margin;
none passes one point. AWM's native browser evaluation is not reproduced, and
no compiler-placed comparator was run (\cref{app:limits}).

Each primary artifact was admitted at 92/92 zero-violation calibration groups,
a support-sufficiency certificate (\cref{sec:method}) that is compiler-wide for
issue-type routing (one candidate at calibration) and per-candidate for
PR-outcome and backlog routing (two; \cref{cor:frozen}, \cref{app:config});
\cref{tab:certificates} lists every admitted artifact. The one certificate whose event includes the continuation comes from a
post-cutoff PR-outcome cohort (pull requests created after the snapshot's last
day; \cref{app:limits}): the re-derived artifact
dispatched on 132 fresh calibration groups and the graded task contract missed
0, a one-sided bound of 0.0173 under the single rule pre-registered for that
study and 0.035 under the primary grid. The certified population is open and
merged pull requests (84 and 48 groups; no closed-unmerged ones); the 60 fresh
test records (20 per class) pass under every condition, and day- and
author-level units give 0.0228 and 0.0267, with their independence unverified.

What the statistical stage adds at runtime is measurable, and null on these
candidates: full statistical admission, support-only admission, and
deterministic guards alone give identical routing and evidence on all 2{,}724
record--model cases of the AWM comparison, and the score $q$ is constant within
each family because the entry state is a single record number
(\cref{app:generalization-gate}). The deterministic barriers do the admission
work; the gate contributes its sample floor, which is what refuses the thin
benchmarks of \cref{sec:res-refusal} and, on a second provider, backlog
routing. On \code{gpt-6-luna} and Anthropic \code{claude-sonnet-5}, re-discovery from
each model's own traces on the same cohorts re-derives the identical programs
on two of the three families and refuses backlog routing (on Sonnet at 91 and
90 of 92 groups, the floor binding), with four compiled-only excerpt misses on
Sonnet (\cref{app:limits}).

\subsection{A public benchmark where \method helps: BIRD}
\label{sec:res-bird}

A live SQL agent on BIRD~\citep{li2023bird}, a public text-to-SQL benchmark,
shows where \method helps (\cref{app:bird}): on the five dev databases with at least 146 questions,
\method retires all five when the agent picks its tables per question, and
admits the schema-first opening on all five. On 150 sealed questions the
compiled agent dispatched every time, answered 97 correctly against 94 (McNemar
$p=0.45$), and cut requests 44.9\% and latency 29.4\% against a warm rerun of the
unchanged agent. Retrospective all-scheduled counts are 93 against 91 of 150
on a second model, and 402 against 403 of 630 on 21 of 26 training databases,
with 49.6\% and 45.8\% fewer requests on completed pairs. A conservative paired 95\% interval for the training difference is
$[-3.27,+2.96]$ points under i.i.d.\ pairs; \cref{tab:bird-quality-sensitivity}
reports database sensitivity. A second provider's agent never ran a fixed
opening and was refused (\cref{tab:bird-extensions}).

\emph{Thin evidence.} NESTFUL, the largest trace corpus the compiler ingests here
(1{,}415 traces), passes provenance, synthesis, and replay with \emph{no} wrong
execution, yet its best family reaches $n_{\max}=26<92$, so every family retires
at stage 6 (\cref{tab:funnel}); API-Bank and BFCL v4
retire the same way at 8 and 15 (\cref{tab:funnel-all}).

\emph{Admissible, rarely dispatchable.} AppWorld's gold solutions reach
$n_{\max}=136$, and \method admits one argument-free two-call artifact at 92/92
zero-violation groups (\cref{tab:certificates}). Yet across 8{,}190
released trajectories it is entry-eligible in $37.9\%$, almost always for
full-code agents and almost never for ReAct and plan-and-execute, which open by
reading API documentation (\cref{tab:appworld-dispatch}); admitting that read as
a prologue recovers only 2 more of 4{,}680 (\cref{app:limits}).

\subsection{Further studies, reported in the appendix}
\label{sec:res-more}

\emph{Newer records within a snapshot} (\cref{app:breadth}). On a two-read
PR-outcome task over five frozen repositories, \method completes 120/120 exact
contracts across four and retires \code{pytorch/pytorch} at admission (no
calibration group accepted at any threshold); open-class records abstain on
the induced \code{pr.state} hull, so requests fall 44.4\% (66.7\% where
dispatched), and a class-balanced rerun with \code{pytorch/pytorch} reaches
180/180 at 66.7\% fewer requests.

\emph{Live recovery} (\cref{app:live-recovery}). A four-arm study of a
three-comment prefetch with equal recovery tools removes request-scope
admission (the entry hull on the requested comment limit) or completeness
checking (the verifier clause that the prefetched page covers that request)
one at a time over 2{,}304 scheduled episodes. On 72 confirmation issue groups
with constructed requests the scope guard raises success from 186/216 to
205/216 ($+8.80$ points, descriptive 95\% interval $[3.70,13.89]$); the
completeness check adds nothing measurable (204/216 versus 205/216 under capped
responses; the agent repairs 22/27 incomplete prefetches); and full does not
beat baseline: mechanism evidence under hand-specified contracts, not a
live-answer certificate.
