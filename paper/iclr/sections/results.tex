\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Compilation reduces model work on recurrent GitHub workflows and schema-first
BIRD openings; the trace benchmarks expose support and dispatch limits.
\Cref{tab:evidence-scope} separates these measurements from the certificates.

Across the 90 held-out records, \method satisfies 90/90 exact contracts and the
unchanged agent 89/90 (\cref{tab:primary}), reducing provider requests by
66.6\%, tokens by 63.1\%, latency by 64.2\%, and cost by 58.7\% (per-record
values and bootstrap intervals in \cref{tab:absolute-resources,tab:reduction-ci},
\cref{app:stats}); issue-type routing, a two-read prefix, is the low end of
every range. These are marginal per-episode savings within one snapshot: every
artifact pins its snapshot digest and abstains on any other (a new snapshot is
recompiled), and discovery cost alone breaks even after 411, 183, and 181
episodes per family (\cref{app:stats}); savings must repay that cost within
each compatible snapshot's lifetime. The discordant pair is one
baseline-only miss (backlog record 5189, an ungrounded excerpt) against zero
compiled-only misses (McNemar $p=1$; one-sided 95\% bound on compiled-only
discordance 3.3\%); a rerun of the 30 records scored 30/30 in every arm
(\cref{app:limits}), so no accuracy difference is detected; equivalence and
noninferiority were not established. Programs hand-written with full workflow
knowledge also reach 90/90 and tie on requests. Pooled across families, they
use fewer interfaces and tokens and cost marginally less, while compilation has
lower latency (\cref{tab:manual-resources}): we claim discovery and admission,
not runtime dominance.

\paragraph{What simpler compilation achieves.}
On the sealed 30-record issue cohort, recurrence-only three-read replay and
\method both satisfy 30/30 contracts, but replay uses one model request
against \method's two (\cref{tab:recurrence-only}). Grounding refuses the
varying \code{comments.limit} argument; that conservatism buys no measured
quality improvement here. This is a whole-system comparison with different
evidence packaging, not an isolated guard intervention. A matched compiler
comparison including rejected candidates remains unrun (\cref{sec:discussion}).

Against an automated reuse baseline, a separately frozen three-workflow,
two-model comparison with an adapted offline Agent Workflow
Memory~\citep{wang2025awm} attempts all 8{,}172 episodes on 1{,}362 fresh
records, retaining one baseline provider failure (\cref{tab:awm};
\cref{app:matched-reuse,app:generalization-gate}). \method uses 50--75\% fewer
requests and 36.9--79.1\% less estimated deployment cost than adapted AWM,
whose induced workflow text leaves every step a model decision. Five of six
cells pass the registered five-point accuracy noninferiority margin with
six-comparison correction; second-model backlog is inconclusive (142/144 versus
143/144; loss bound 5.86\%). Only the two PR cells pass a two-point margin;
none passes one point. Five points is a research tolerance, not a safety
threshold. AWM's native browser evaluation is not reproduced, and
no compiler-placed comparator was run (\cref{app:limits}).

On \code{gpt-6-luna} and Anthropic \code{claude-sonnet-5}, re-discovery from
each model's own traces on the same cohorts re-derives the identical programs
on two of the three families and refuses backlog routing (on Sonnet at 91 and
90 of 92 groups, the floor binding), with four compiled-only excerpt misses on
Sonnet; transferring the retained artifacts to \code{gpt-6-luna} keeps the
structure with one compiled-only miss in 90 (\cref{app:limits}).

\subsection{A public benchmark where \method helps: BIRD}
\label{sec:res-bird}

A live SQL agent on BIRD~\citep{li2023bird}, a public text-to-SQL benchmark,
shows where \method helps (\cref{app:bird}): on the five dev databases with at
least 146 questions, \method retires all five when the agent picks its tables
per question, and admits the schema-first opening on all five. On 150 sealed
questions the compiled agent dispatched every time,
answered 97 correctly against 94 (McNemar $p=0.45$), and cut requests 44.9\%
and latency 29.4\% against a warm rerun of the unchanged agent. Retrospective all-scheduled
counts are 93 against 91 of 150 on a second model, and 402 against 403 of 630
on 21 of 26 training databases. Requests fall 49.6\% and 45.8\% on 150 and 629
completed pairs, respectively. A conservative paired 95\% interval for the
training difference is $[-3.27,+2.96]$ points under i.i.d.\ pairs;
\cref{tab:bird-quality-sensitivity} reports database sensitivity. A second
provider's agent never ran a fixed opening and was refused
(\cref{tab:bird-extensions}).

\emph{Thin evidence.} NESTFUL, the largest trace corpus the compiler ingests here
(1{,}415 traces), passes provenance, synthesis, and replay with \emph{no} wrong
execution, yet its best family reaches $n_{\max}=26<92$, so every family retires
at stage 6 (\cref{tab:funnel}); API-Bank and BFCL v4 retire the same way at 8
and 15 (\cref{tab:funnel-all}).

\emph{Admissible, rarely dispatchable.} AppWorld's gold solutions reach
$n_{\max}=136$, and \method admits one argument-free two-call artifact at 92/92
zero-violation groups ($U_{\hat\eta}=0.0498$, compiler-wide under one effect
arm, per-candidate under the other; \cref{app:external}). Yet across 8{,}190
released trajectories it is entry-eligible in $37.9\%$, almost always for
full-code agents and almost never for ReAct and plan-and-execute, which open by
reading API documentation (\cref{tab:appworld-dispatch}). Allowing that read as
a prologue recovers only 2 more of 4{,}680 (\cref{app:limits}).

\emph{What is certified} (\cref{app:certificates}). Each primary artifact was
admitted at 92/92 zero-violation calibration groups ($U_{\hat\eta}=0.0498$,
conditional on i.i.d.\ groups), a support-sufficiency certificate that is
compiler-wide for issue-type routing (one candidate at calibration) and
per-candidate for PR-outcome and backlog routing (two; corrected $U=0.0569$, so
compiler-wide only at $\alpha\ge0.057$; \cref{cor:frozen}); \cref{tab:certificates}
lists every admitted artifact, including the one end-to-end certificate, from a
post-cutoff PR-outcome cohort on which the re-derived artifact dispatched on
132 fresh groups and missed 0 task contracts (one-sided bound 0.0173 under the
single pre-registered rule, 0.035 under the primary grid). What the statistical
stage adds at runtime is measurable, and null on these candidates: full
statistical admission, support-only admission, and deterministic guards give
identical routing and evidence on all 2{,}724 record--model cases of the AWM
comparison. This conditional-on-admission ablation does not test whether
compilation excluded bad candidates. The sample floor refuses the thin
benchmarks of \cref{sec:res-refusal} and second-provider backlog routing.

\emph{Newer records within a snapshot} (\cref{app:breadth}). On a two-read
PR-outcome task over five frozen repositories, \method completes 120/120 exact
contracts across four and retires \code{pytorch/pytorch} at admission (no
calibration group accepted at any threshold); open-class records abstain on
the induced \code{pr.state} hull, so requests fall 44.4\% (66.7\% where
dispatched), and a class-balanced rerun with \code{pytorch/pytorch} reaches
180/180 at 66.7\% fewer requests.

\emph{Live recovery} (\cref{app:live-recovery}). A four-arm study of a
three-comment prefetch with equal recovery tools removes request-scope
admission (the entry hull on the requested comment limit) or completeness
checking (the verifier clause that the prefetched page covers that request)
one at a time over 2{,}304 scheduled episodes. On 72 confirmation issue groups
with constructed requests the scope guard raises success from 186/216 to
205/216 ($+8.80$ points, descriptive 95\% interval $[3.70,13.89]$); removing
the completeness check yields 204/216 versus 205/216 under capped responses
(the agent repairs 22/27 incomplete prefetches), so its benefit is unresolved;
and full does not beat baseline: mechanism evidence under hand-specified
contracts, not a live-answer certificate.
