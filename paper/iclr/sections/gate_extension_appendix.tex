\subsection{Sequential calibration extension of the issue gate}
\label{app:gate-extension}

The retired 184-group study above was extended once, under a protocol
registered after that retirement had been observed
(\path{paper/results/graded_issue_gate/extension_protocol.md}; sealed cohort
\path{extension_preflight.json}; provider-free audit
\path{audit_extension.json}). The fresh calibration cohort is every remaining
eligible issue of the pinned snapshot after excluding all record numbers
retained under \path{paper/results/} and \path{experiments/} (the original rule
scanned only the former; the 474 original and 90 pilot issues are disjoint from
the 1,728 \path{experiments/} numbers), the pilot cohort, and the original 474:
127 issues (plain text 80, bare URL 43, Markdown link 4), a size forced by
pool exhaustion rather than chosen. The score, eleven-point grid,
$\alpha=0.05$, and $\delta=0.10$ are the frozen v2 ones, hash-pinned; the 90
test issues stayed sealed until the pooled threshold was fixed. An earlier
attempt the same day failed on provider authentication before any episode
completed (\path{extension_abort.json}; no cost). The executed run attempted
397 episodes at an estimated \$0.19.

\Cref{tab:gate-extension} gives the full sweep. The candidate dispatched on all
127 fresh issues with 2 final factual-task errors. On the fresh set alone no
threshold is admissible (best $U=0.0682$ at $\eta=0.30$). On the pooled 311
groups (4 errors) six grid points are admissible---$\eta=0.02$ (117 groups, no
error, $U=0.0394$) and $\eta\in\{0.20,0.25,0.30,0.40,0.50\}$---while
$\eta=0.05$--$0.17$ are not, so the admissible set is not an interval; the
registered rule takes the largest, $\eta=0.50$: 261/311 groups (83.9\%
coverage), 4 errors, $U=0.0443$. The descriptive split at $\eta=0.02$ places
all four errors above the threshold (4/194 vs.\ 0/117), but a one-sided Fisher
test gives $p=0.150$: no evidence of graded selectivity.

Under $\eta=0.50$ the learned routing sent 76/90 test issues to the compiled
runtime (all 76 actually dispatched) and 14 to the unchanged agent. Final-task
errors were 2/90 for the unchanged agent, 3/90 for learned routing (one-sided
95\% bound 8.4\%), and 5/90 for the support-only accept-all routing. Two of
the three learned-arm errors followed compiled dispatch (issues 4149 and 7291;
2/76, bound 8.1\%), the adverse events this protocol foregrounds. Paired with
the unchanged agent, 85 issues were correct under both, 3 only under the
unchanged agent, 2 only under learned routing, and none wrong under both.
Learned routing used 132 provider requests against 360 for the unchanged
agent on the same 90 issues, with the pre-dispatch read counted for every arm.
This extension shows that the frozen score can certify intermediate coverage
on this pooled set at the registered level. It is a single-repository,
sequential result on a mixture of two sampling frames (quota-enriched 184 plus
the natural 127 remainder); it does not establish graded selectivity,
cross-repository generalization, or a quality benefit, and the 90-issue test
cannot confirm the 5\% risk level on its own.
