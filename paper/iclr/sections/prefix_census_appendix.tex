\subsection{Prefix census over released agent trajectories}
\label{app:prefix-census}

The introduction asks how often deployed agents open with a compilable
read-only evidence path. No deployment traces are public, so this appendix
measures the nearest proxy: released corpora of \emph{executed} multi-step
tool-using trajectories from three scaffolds the paper does not otherwise
touch, re-measured beside the two substrates it already ingests, all under one
set of definitions and with no model in the loop
(\code{paper/scripts/prefix\_census.py}; sources, catalogs and aggregates
under \code{paper/results/prefix\_census/}). It is descriptive: nothing here
is synthesized, calibrated or dispatched, and prefix eligibility is not
admission.

\paragraph{Corpora.}
(i)~\emph{OpenHands / SWE-rebench}: 67{,}074 OpenHands~v0.54.0
\citep{wang2024openhands} CodeAct trajectories with native function calling
(Qwen3-Coder-480B) over 6{,}306 real issues in 1{,}823
repositories~\citep{trofimova2025openhands}. (ii)~\emph{SWE-agent}: 80{,}036
legacy SWE-agent~\citep{yang2024sweagent} text-action ReAct trajectories
(fine-tuned Llama-3 action generators, three sizes) over 3{,}591 real
issues~\citep{nebius2024sweagent}. (iii)~$\tau$-bench: the
1{,}980 released historical runs of gpt-4o and claude-3.5-sonnet over the 165
airline and retail tasks, a function-calling conversational agent in a
non-coding domain~\citep{yao2024taubench}. (iv)~NESTFUL gold sequences and (v)~AppWorld's 28
released baseline runs (8{,}190 trajectories, four architectures), the
substrates of \cref{sec:res-refusal} and \cref{tab:appworld-dispatch}. Each
corpus is pinned by revision and SHA-256 in
\code{paper/results/datasets/*/source\_manifest.json}; raw corpora are parsed
in isolation, never executed, and not committed. Candidates rejected and why
(SWE-smith, the OpenHands SWE-Gym releases, $\tau^2$-bench, AgentBench and
WebArena logs, ToolBench's archive) are recorded in the directory's README.

\paragraph{Definitions, identical for every corpus.}
The first model boundary is the first agent turn after the task prompt;
normalized position~0 is the first tool call. The entry state is the structured
task record the scaffold's prompt is filled from (instance, repository,
problem statement, workspace path; for $\tau$-bench the user's opening
utterance), seeded as pseudo-producer~0 as in \cref{alg:patg}. A
\emph{read-only opening} is the run of consecutive calls from position~0 whose
signed catalog entry is compilable; a new user utterance, an unknown effect, a
write, an approval-gated tool or a missing capability ends it. A
\emph{groundable opening} is the longest canonical window of the window-mining
stage of \cref{alg:compile} that starts at the first boundary, at the
registered parameters (depth~2, $\kappa=3$,
$w\in[2,12]$, $b_{\min}=2$, entry schema \code{inputs}): every slot a literal
invariant across traces, or grounded to the entry state or a prior result by a
bounded transform, exactly \cref{eq:prefix}. Families are canonical hashes of
opening windows with support counted in distinct tasks; $n_{\max}$ is compared
with the 92-group floor of \cref{eq:cp}. Only the first thirteen calls are
ingested ($w_{\max}+1$), which cannot change a window that starts at the first
boundary. One signed catalog per corpus under
\code{benchmarks/contracts/effects/} carries one justification per tool; editor sub-commands are distinct entries
(\code{str\_replace\_editor.view} is a read, \code{create} a write), the model-side
\code{think} tool is a barrier, and every shell invocation is \code{UNKNOWN}. Each
released corpus has exactly one labelled sensitivity arm, never pooled with the
primary row: admitting single-utility shell invocations (\code{ls}, \code{cat},
\code{grep}, \code{find} without executing flags, read \code{git} subcommands)
as reads for the two coding corpora, and, for $\tau$-bench, measuring from the
first tool-issuing turn with the user utterances before it folded into the
entry state, the analogue of the AppWorld prologue relaxation of
\cref{tab:appworld-prologue}.

\paragraph{Findings.}
\Cref{tab:prefix-census} gives the census. Read-only openings of two or more
calls are a minority everywhere but one architecture: 16.3\% of OpenHands
trajectories, 32.5\% of SWE-agent trajectories (47.3\% with shell reads
admitted), 3.9\% of $\tau$-bench runs under the strict first-boundary rule
(51.7\% once the identity-collecting user turns are folded into entry state),
and 41.0\% of AppWorld's released trajectories, where the split is by
architecture (full-code 99.96\%, iterative function calling 71.4\%, ReAct
5.7\%, plan-and-execute 2.1\%), consistent with \cref{tab:appworld-dispatch}.
The first barrier explains most of the remainder and is a scaffold habit rather
than a workload property: 87.5\% of OpenHands trajectories open with the
model-side \code{think} tool, 47.0\% of SWE-agent trajectories open by creating
a reproduction script (a write), 99.7\% of $\tau$-bench openings are cut by
the next user turn, and 45.9\% of AppWorld trajectories open with the
undeclared API-documentation call.

Groundability under \cref{eq:prefix} separates the scaffolds further.
On OpenHands, 79\% of read-only openings are groundable (12.8\% of all
trajectories, median three calls): the opening is a run of directory and file
views whose paths derive from the workspace path in the entry state, and the
largest family spans 752 tasks, above the 92-group floor, with eleven families
at or above it. On SWE-agent only 6\% of read-only openings are groundable
(1.9\% of trajectories): the scaffold's first \code{open}, \code{search\_dir}
and \code{find\_file} arguments are model-chosen paths and search terms that
no depth-2 transform of the issue text reproduces (24{,}399 openings blocked
on an ungrounded slot, led by \code{open.path} and \code{search\_dir.search\_term});
the two families at the floor (187 tasks each) are two-call search openings.
On $\tau$-bench the strict measure grounds 3.0\% of runs, all from the
claude-3.5-sonnet runs that query a reservation named in the opening
utterance; the folded arm grounds 16.6\%, and its largest family (17 tasks)
cannot reach the floor because the benchmark has 165 tasks. NESTFUL's openings
are read-only by construction and 86.6\% groundable (the rest have ambiguous
slots); its largest opening family spans 76 of 1{,}415 sequences, against 26
for whole sequences in \cref{tab:funnel}. In short, the one-in-six to
one-in-three prevalence of read-only openings, the lower prevalence of
groundable ones, and the architecture dependence all match what
\cref{sec:res-refusal} found on AppWorld alone.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
These are benchmark runs, not deployments; prevalence in production agents
remains unmeasured in the literal sense, and these corpora over-represent
software engineering. The catalogs are ours, are not backed by a mutation
audit as the BFCL and AppWorld catalogs are, and are conservative by design:
every shell command is unknown, so the primary rows are lower bounds on
read-only openings and the shell arm is an upper bound under a plausible
declaration. The SWE-agent scaffold's editor window is implicit state that
\code{goto}, \code{scroll} and the no-argument \code{search\_file} read without
an argument slot, which \cref{eq:prefix} cannot see. Observations longer than
32{,}000 characters are cut, which can only lose producers. Support is counted
in tasks, and trajectories of one task across models or trials are not
independent. Finally, an opening family above the floor is a candidate for
\cref{alg:compile}, nothing more: synthesis, the replay contract and the
calibrated gate were not run on any of these corpora.
