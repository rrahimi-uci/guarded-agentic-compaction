Tool-using agents often call a language model at every step, even when they
repeat the same read\nobreakdash-only lookups. Replacing that repeated reasoning with a
fixed program could cut cost, but repetition alone does not make it safe:
arguments may depend on earlier results, tools may have side effects, and
replaying the same calls may still change the final answer.

We introduce \emph{guarded agentic compaction} (\method), which compiles
recurring read\nobreakdash-only workflow openings into deterministic programs and otherwise
leaves the agent unchanged. \method grounds arguments in inputs, prior results,
or observed invariant literals; treats writes, approvals, handoffs and unknown
effects as hard stops; checks inputs and outputs; and admits programs through
an exact finite-sample test under independent-group assumptions.

On three GitHub workflows, the compiled agent solves 90 of 90 held-out tasks
versus 89 for the original agent, with about two-thirds fewer model calls.
A separately frozen comparison with adapted offline Agent Workflow Memory
spans three workflows, two models and 1,362 fresh records (8,172 attempted
episodes). Estimated deployment cost falls 36.9--79.1\% and model requests
50--75\%. Five of six comparisons meet a preregistered five-percentage-point
accuracy noninferiority margin with multiplicity correction; the smaller
second-model backlog cohort is inconclusive. Only two comparisons meet a
two-point margin. Statistical, support-only and deterministic gates produce
identical routing and evidence on all selected cases, providing no incremental
runtime gate benefit on these already admitted candidates.

On BIRD, a schema-first SQL agent's opening compiles on 26 of 31 databases,
with 45--50\% fewer model calls and no detected accuracy loss; question-specific
table selection is refused. NESTFUL and AppWorld show no measured deployment
benefit. On 132 pull requests created after the data snapshot, a re-derived
program made no end-to-end error (rate below 2\% at 90\% confidence). A live
safeguard ablation shows that request-scope checks help on constructed tasks,
while the agent often recovers from incomplete evidence without a completeness
check, and earlier drift tests show no protection.
The evidence supports bounded compilation of recurring workflows, not
learned-gate superiority, universal accuracy preservation, or cross-domain
savings. Most certificates concern replay rather than final answers;
task, author and time independence remain assumptions.
