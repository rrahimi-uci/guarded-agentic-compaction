Thank you for the careful assessment. Weaknesses follow in your order; your three questions are answered in Part 2.

**[W1] Metadata workloads; handwritten ties; automation value unquantified**
Answer: Agreed on the workloads: the primary families are metadata classification and verbatim extraction, and the handwritten program matches them (90/90 contracts, equal requests; Table 9 in the reviewed version, now Table 14). We treat that program as the oracle: the claim is that the compiler recovers it from traces without human input where support exists and refuses where it does not, at a measured discovery cost that breaks even after 411, 183 and 181 episodes per family. Matching that program is the intended result: GAC automates what an engineer would write, and the guard makes the automation safe. The end-to-end study supports this across eleven repositories and 3,560 fresh groups: the compiled, unchanged and hand-written arms meet 3,510, 3,507 and 3,504 contracts, and the compiled and hand-written arms both cut cost by 73.6%. Where the guard cannot justify a program it refuses (node and cpython are retired at n = 400). The cross-repository and post-cutoff studies use PR-outcome because it is the family with enough fresh records; they test transfer and certification, not task difficulty.
Changed: New table "What the compiler decided without human input" at the start of Section 5 (admit or retire across families, repositories, models, providers and benchmarks). BIRD (Section 5.2), new since the reviewed version, adds a live agent that writes SQL after the compiled schema-first opening.
Evidence: `paper/results/bird/`: the opening compiles on 26/31 databases; on 150 sealed questions the compiled agent answers 97 and the unchanged agent 94, with 44.9% fewer requests.
Still open: The engineering value of automatic discovery over hand-writing is not quantified, and no adaptive-task advantage is claimed (Q3).

**[W2] Guards and learned gate not individually attributed; drift unexecuted**
Answer: Partly agreed, and the missing studies now exist. (a) Recurrence-only replay still ties GAC at 30/30 with one fewer request (Table 19 in the reviewed version, now Table 33); a refusal is not counted as an avoided error. (b) A live-recovery study removes one mechanism at a time over 2,304 scheduled episodes: the scope guard raises success from 186/216 to 205/216 (+8.80 points, descriptive 95% interval [3.70, 13.89]); the completeness check's benefit is unresolved (204/216 versus 205/216). (c) Drift was run three ways: provider-free (144 paired groups) and recorded replay (89 records) are null with zero wrong answers in every arm, and continuation-graded drift is adverse (39/60 wrong in both arms, 40 under the corrected grader), so the verifier bought abstention only. (d) The learned gate is no longer claimed: full statistical, support-only and deterministic routing are identical on all 2,724 record-model cases (Table 23 in the reviewed version is now Table 38).
Changed: Section 5.4 "Mechanism evidence"; Section 3 Stage 6 states that the score is implemented, every admitted operating point is full coverage, and graded selection is not claimed; Appendices G and I.
