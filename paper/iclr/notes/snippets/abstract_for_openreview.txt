Tool-using agents pay for a model decision at every tool boundary. When a workflow recurs, an engineer can hand-write a program for its opening, but must write, check, and maintain it, and recurrence alone does not make it safe. Guarded agentic compaction (GAC) automates that step safely: it compiles an agent's opening read-only calls into a deterministic program only when every argument is grounded in observed state, every effect is declared read-only, and the program starts at the first step; otherwise it refuses, and admits one only when enough calibration examples, assumed independent, support a finite-sample error bound. The target is the program an engineer would write, found without being told the workflow. On three GitHub workflows the compiled agent satisfies 90/90 held-out task contracts, as hand-written programs do, against 89/90 for the unchanged agent, with 66.6% fewer model requests. Against adapted offline Agent Workflow Memory on 1,344 fresh records, five of six workflow and model cells meet a five-point accuracy noninferiority margin at 36.9-79.1% lower estimated cost. On BIRD text-to-SQL, a schema-first opening compiles on 26/31 databases; three tool-trace benchmarks are refused for insufficient calibration support, and AppWorld's program is rarely eligible. Most bounds cover replay on selected populations; pull-request bounds cover final answers: no failure on 132 pull requests opened after the snapshot (90% upper bound 0.0173). A later study samples one pull request per author before any outcome filter and grades final task contracts: 3 misses in 760 groups across four repositories, each admitted (bounds 0.023-0.042). Of seven repositories added at 400 groups, five are admitted and two retired; over all eleven, compiled, unchanged and hand-written arms meet 3,510, 3,507 and 3,504 of 3,560 contracts. Replay bounds do not cover unfiltered deployment, two primary families miss the selection-adjusted 5% target, and we claim no benefit on adaptive tasks or from a learned dispatch gate.
