\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The supported claim is narrow: repeated read-only prefixes can become guarded
programs that preserve held-out contracts on the tested GitHub families and
retire when evidence is inadequate.

\paragraph{Gate maturity is the main scientific risk.}
\Cref{prop:admission} is per-candidate, not compiler-wide over
\cref{alg:compile}'s adaptive search unless that search is frozen
(\cref{cor:frozen}); the primary GitHub families do not freeze, so a
multiplicity repair would raise their requirement further (\cref{app:config}).
The gate is also empirically step-like at $n_\eta\ge 92$, AppWorld included: it
\emph{refuses} correctly far better than $q$ \emph{discriminates}, and
refitting $q$ for a genuine frontier is the key follow-up.  A pre-registered
protocol for testing this at scale has since been partially executed
(\cref{app:prospective}): across four repositories and 240 held-out triples,
a support-only comparator that shares every statistical mechanism except the
risk budget is indistinguishable from the learned gate, and neither produces
graded coverage --- confirmation of the step-gate finding at four times the
prior cross-repository scale, not yet its resolution.

\paragraph{Group independence is assumed, not established.}
\Cref{prop:admission} needs i.i.d.\ groups. Here each group is one record from
one snapshot with \code{min\_days}=\code{min\_principals}=1, and AppWorld's
task-level groups share a scenario's supervisor and database, so neither corpus
establishes exchangeability and the second inherits the conditional rather than
repairing it (\cref{app:limits}). The 8{,}190 released AppWorld trajectories also
repeat tasks across models, architectures, and runs; their structural-eligibility
rate is a descriptive deployment diagnostic, not an independent-sample estimate.

\paragraph{External validity, dispatchability, and competitiveness.}
The richer evidence comes from one revision-pinned repository and one
provider/model family, and the cross-repository extension is a narrower,
template-tied task. Admission is also not dispatchability: a prefix is only worth
compiling for an architecture that issues one, which neither
\cref{prop:admission} nor the guard tests. We establish no superiority under
drift or across providers and do not price manual authoring or maintenance,
though a live compression-comparator ablation and a pre-registered
drift-parity protocol narrow both gaps (\cref{app:limits}).
Finally, exact task contracts do not certify a downstream model continuation, and
writes, handoffs, hosted tools, undeclared effects, and removed-turn guardrails
lie outside \cref{prop:admission} entirely (\cref{app:limits}).
